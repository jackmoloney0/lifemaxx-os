Decision-Making

14 entries

On thinking in probabilities, embracing uncertainty, and separating luck from skill.

Thinking in Bets
Outcome ≠ Decision Quality— We tend to equate the quality of a decision with the quality of its outcome. Never associate getting "lucky" with good decisions.
Hidden Biases— Beliefs drive how we process information. We have a blind spot for our own biases.
Self-Serving Attribution— We blame our bad outcomes on luck, but our peers' bad outcomes on their choices. Our wins are skill; their wins are luck.
Mistakes as Learning— Instead of feeling bad when admitting a mistake, recognise you might be missing a learning opportunity.
Multi-Causal Outcomes— Outcomes are rarely attributed to a single cause. You don't have to be on the defensive side of every negative outcome.
Pre-determined Loss Limits— Set your limits before the game starts. Seek diversity in decision-making.
Source Independence— Don't discredit an idea because of who or where it came from. Imagine it coming from someone you deeply respect.

Cognitive Biases & Heuristics

16 entries

The invisible forces that warp how we perceive, decide, and remember.

Poor Charlie's Almanack — Psychological Models
Incentive-Caused Bias— Incentives override ethics, logic, and truth.
Loving Tendency— We distort reality in favour of people and brands we like.
Hating Tendency— We irrationally dismiss ideas from people we don't like.
Doubt-Avoidance— We prefer certainty, even false certainty, to ambiguity.
Reciprocation Tendency— We feel compelled to return favours — can be manipulated by small ones.
Mere-Association— We transfer feelings from one thing to another without logical basis.
Authority-Misinfluence— We overweight opinions from perceived authorities.
Social Proof— We imitate behaviours of others.
Confirmation Bias— We seek evidence confirming existing beliefs.
Narrative Fallacy— We impose stories on entirely random or complex systems.
Over-optimism & Overconfidence— Systematic miscalibration of our own certainty.
Thinking, Fast & Slow — Heuristics & Biases
System 1 vs System 2— Fast intuitive thinking vs. slow deliberate reasoning.
Availability Heuristic— Judging probability by ease of recall.
Anchoring— Relying heavily on an initial reference point. "High-balling an offer."
Overweighting Vivid Examples— Terrorist attacks and extreme events cause us to overweigh probability of occurrence.
Planning Fallacy— Consistently underestimating time and cost, caused by belief that statistics don't apply to our team.
Risk Aversion in Gains— Preference for certain rewards. 80% chance of $1000 or $650 certain?
Risk Seeking in Losses— When losing, we trust the smallest probabilities to prevent a certain loss.
Focusing Illusion— Tendency to overestimate whatever you're currently focused on.
Peak-End Rule— Only peak pleasure/pain and the very end of an experience are remembered. Creates a gap between the remembering self and experiencing self.
Thinking, Fast & Slow
Nothing in life is as important as you think it is while you are thinking about it.
Thinking, Fast & Slow
Confidence is a feeling, not a fact.
Thinking, Fast & Slow
Losses loom larger than gains.

People & Influence

3 entries

On handling people with grace, resolving conflict, and leading with empathy.

How to Win Friends & Influence People — Handling People
Never Criticise— Don't criticise, condemn, or complain.
Sincere Appreciation— Be honest and sincere in appreciation.
Arouse Wants— Arouse wants in others to aid your objective.
How to Win Friends & Influence People — Humility & Conflict
Avoid Arguments— Always avoid arguments. Respect other opinions; never say "You're wrong."
Admit Mistakes— When wrong, admit it empathetically.
Warmth over Force— Be gentle and friendly to get what you want. "The sun can make you take off your coat more quickly than the wind."
How to Win Friends & Influence People — Leadership
Lead with Praise— Begin with praise and appreciation to soften criticism.
"And" not "But"— Be indirect with criticism. Replace "really good but..." with "really good, and..."
Be Human— Talk about your own mistakes before pointing out someone else's.
Ask, Don't Order— Ask questions instead of giving orders.
